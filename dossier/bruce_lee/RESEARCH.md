## 1. TIMELINE (era by era, with physique notes)

**1940 — born.** Lee Jun-fan, born Nov 27, 1940, San Francisco, while his father toured with the Cantonese opera; raised in Hong Kong. His superstitious mother first called him *Sai-fon* ("small phoenix"), a feminine name meant to fool spirits. [WP-BL, citing Lee 1989 p.20 and A&E *Bruce Lee: The Immortal Dragon*, 2002]
- Photos in refs: `photo_infant_3months.jpg`, `photo_1940s_child_with_parents.jpg` (both PD, Wikimedia Commons).

**1940s–50s — child actor, street kid.** Introduced to Hong Kong film as a child actor by his father Lee Hoi-chuen; his teens were full of neighborhood and rooftop street fights. [WP-BL] Child-actor photo from *The Birth of Mankind* (1946): `photo_1946_child_actor.jpg` (PD, Commons).

**1953 — Ip Man.** Began Wing Chun under Ip Man (introduced by William Cheung); after about a year most other students refused to train with him because of his part-European ancestry, so he got private-leaning attention. [WP-BL, citing Polly 2018]

**1958 — proof he could fight.** Won the Hong Kong schools boxing tournament, knocking out reigning champ Gary Elms in the final — and won the Crown Colony Cha-Cha Championship the same year. [WP-BL, citing Lee (Linda) 1989 p.30]

**1959–1963 — Seattle.** Sent to America in 1959; University of Washington from 1961; began teaching "Jun Fan Gung Fu" to a racially mixed group (Jesse Glover first); invented the one-inch punch in this period; became obsessed with Muhammad Ali and Sugar Ray Robinson footage. [WP-BL, citing Polly 2018 pp.92–100, 114]
- Physique note: still the light, whip-thin wing chun build; the systematic bodybuilding comes later (see 1965 turn).

**1964 — the two detonations.**
- *Long Beach, August 1964*: at Ed Parker's International Karate Championships he performed **two-finger push-ups** (thumb + index of one hand) and the **one-inch punch** on volunteer Bob Baker. Baker: "I told Bruce not to do this type of demonstration again. When he punched me that last time, I had to stay home from work because the pain in my chest was unbearable." [WP-BL, citing Vaughn & Lee, *The Legendary Bruce Lee* (1986) p.21 — archive.org: legendarybrucele0000unse] Met Jhoon Rhee there (traded side-kick lessons for the non-telegraphic punch). [WP-BL, citing *Black Belt* May 1996 pp.39–43]
- *Oakland, late winter 1964*: the **Wong Jack-man fight** (private, in his Oakland studio). Accounts conflict — Linda Lee: ~3 minutes, decisive; Wong and witness William Chen: 20–25 minutes, no knockdown. What is not disputed is the aftermath: Lee's fists were swollen from hitting Wong's head, and he told *Black Belt*: **"I realized Wing Chun was not too practical and began to alter my way of fighting."** [Wikipedia "Wong Jack-man", citing Michael Dorgan, "Bruce Lee's Toughest Fight," *Official Karate*, July 1980; and WP-BL, citing Polly 2018 pp.152–157]

**1965 — the science era begins (documented in his own letters).**
- Aug 1965, from Hong Kong: "I'm having a gung fu system drawn up — this system is a combination of chiefly Wing Chun, fencing and boxing." [LotD, letter to James Yimm Lee, posted Kowloon Aug 7, 1965]
- Aug 6, 1965: mails James Lee a full class training program (limbering: waist/leg/shoulder/arm/wrist; punching; kicking; techniques) and orders: "Use your own ideas… **the hell with conventional methods and opinions.**" [LotD; page image saved: `doc_letter_1965-08-06_training_program.png`]
- Also 1965: iron-ring resistance for sil lum tao ("I've made 8, 3 pounds each"), calls it "a kind of modernized progressive weight training method"; and "As of today, I haven't yet received the protein…" — protein supplements already in the mail. [LotD, Hong Kong letters, 1965]
- Dec 18, 1965, to equipment-maker George Lee: "My gripping power and forearms have improved greatly — thanks for your wrist roller." (grip machine, dip bar also mentioned) [LotD]
- Weight training itself was seeded by Oakland bodybuilder-student Allen Joe. [LotD, editor's note 9]

**1966–67 — Kato.** *The Green Hornet* (one season). Physique: the lean Kato build — `photo_1967_kato.jpg` (PD, Commons). Names his art **Jeet Kune Do** in 1967 (Chinatown LA school, Dan Inosanto assistant instructor). [WP-BL, citing Polly 2018 p.197]

**1968 — peak systematization.** New Year's resolution letter, January 1968: **"I now train an average of two-and-a-half hours a day, including hand exercises, leg exercises, running, isometrics, stomach exercises, sparring exercises, free-hand exercises."** [LotD, to George Lee, Jan 1968; page image: `doc_letter_1968-01_daily_training_isometrics.png`] Higher-quality Long Beach demo footage is from the 1968 championships (blindfolded chi sao, full-contact bout in headgear). [WP-BL, citing *Black Belt* Dec 1995 pp.78–79]

**1969 — the letter to himself.** "My Definite Chief Aim," signed and dated 1969 (secretly kept; the archive photo carries a SECRET stamp): **"I, Bruce Lee, will be the first highest paid Oriental super star in the United States… Starting 1970 I will achieve world fame and from then onward till the end of 1980 I will have in my possession $10,000,000. I will live the way I please and achieve inner harmony and happiness."** [Scan: `doc_definite_chief_aim_1969.jpg`; transcript per Letters of Note, "My Definite Chief Aim," Mar 16, 2011 — archived: web.archive.org/web/20110319085235/http://www.lettersofnote.com/2011/03/my-definite-chief-aim.html]

**1970 — the back.** "In 1970, Bruce sustained a rather severe injury to his back. His doctors ordered him to discontinue the practice of martial arts and to remain in bed… He stayed in bed, virtually flat on his back for six months, but he couldn't keep his mind from working — the result of which is this book." — Linda Lee, Introduction to ToJKD (1975). [Page image: `doc_tao_jkd_1975_intro_back_injury.png`] Corroborated: "Bruce Lee hurt his lower back quite severely in 1970… his injury preventing him from practicing, Linda had to work evenings in order to help support the family." [LotD, editor's note 29] Wikipedia's *Tao of Jeet Kune Do* article adds: doctors ordered a back brace for 6 months.

**1971–72 — comeback and stardom.** Four *Longstreet* episodes (his martial philosophy written into the scripts); *The Big Boss* (1971) explodes in Asia; *Fist of Fury* and *Way of the Dragon* (1972) break records (~$100M and $130M worldwide gross). Oct 28, 1971, letter to Warner's Ted Ashley on Golden Harvest letterhead: "the last picture I made in Thailand… is on its way to break all records in Hong Kong — **damn the torpedo, full speed ahead!!**" [LotD, facsimile — image: `doc_letter_1971-10-28_golden_harvest_back_injury_note.png`; grosses per WP-BL] **Dec 9, 1971**: *The Pierre Berton Show* interview — described by Wikipedia's article on the show as "the only known interview with Bruce Lee" (see nuance in UNVERIFIED). [WP-BL + Wikipedia "The Pierre Berton Show"]

**1973 — death at 32.** Died July 20, 1973, six days before *Enter the Dragon*'s Hollywood release; cause: cerebral (brain) edema, causes still disputed. [WP-BL, citing Britannica] Physique at the end: 172 cm (5'7½"), 64 kg (~141 lb). [WP-BL, citing "The Last Days of Bruce Lee"] AP wire photo distributed at his death: `photo_1973_ap_death.jpg` (PD, Commons).

**The logs he left:** seven years of day planners, workout diaries and self-measurements were compiled by John Little into AOEHB (1998) — the centerpiece book. Its scans are locked behind archive.org lending (see THE DOCUMENTS).

## 3. TRAINING SYSTEM (the actual logged/published routines, with numbers)

**Daily volume (his own words, Jan 1968):** ~2.5 hours/day: hand exercises, leg exercises, running, isometrics, stomach exercises, sparring, free-hand exercises. [LotD, Jan 1968 letter — image in refs]

**Cardio (published in BLFM pp.16–17):**
- Running: "one of his favorite exercises was running **four miles a day in 24 to 25 minutes**," with tempo changes — long even strides broken by sprints and foot-shuffles; ran anywhere (beach, parks, woods, hills, streets). Took his running shoes on location scouts to India.
- Exercycle: "full speed — **35 to 40 miles an hour continuously for 45 minutes to an hour**," often right after running.
- Jump rope, scheduled into the routine.
[BLFM (Lee & Uyehara, 1977), Basic Training ch.1 "Aerobic Exercises" — page images in refs]

**The circuits ("Sequence Training") — from his own notes as published in ToJKD:**
- Sequence 1 (Mon/Wed/Fri): rope jumping, forward bend, cat stretch, jumping jack, squat, high kick.
- Sequence 2 (Tue/Thu/Sat): groin stretch, side leg raise, jumping squat, shoulder circling, alternate splits, leg stretch.
- Forearm/Waist, Seq 1 (M/W/F): waist twisting, palm-up curl, Roman chair, knee drawing, side bend, palm-down curl. Seq 2 (T/Th/Sa): leg raises, reverse curl, sit-up twist, leverage bar twist, alternate leg raise, wrist roller.
- **Power Training** (his isometric/strength station list): press lockout, press start, rise on toes, pull, squat, shrug, deadlift, quarter squat, frog kick.
- Plus an 11-exercise Fitness Program warm-up (alternate splits, push-ups, running in place, shoulder circling, high kicks, deep knee bends, side-kick raises, twisting sit-ups, waist twisting, leg raises, forward bends) and "everyday opportunities" (park far away, take stairs, balance on one foot dressing).
[ToJKD, "Preliminaries → Training / Supplementary Training" — page images in refs. NOTE: the AOEHB claim that he *logged* these circuits day-by-day 1965–1971 in his planners is book-locked → see UNVERIFIED.]

**Isometrics:** named by Lee himself in the Jan 1968 letter (above); the Power Training list in ToJKD is the station breakdown; BLFM p.82 shows him doing chi-sao pressure on a steering wheel "as though… doing isometric exercise." [LotD; ToJKD; BLFM]

**Grip/forearm obsession:** wrist roller, grip machine, dip bar custom-built by student George Lee to Bruce's drawings; wall-mounted punching pads; a silver "finger bowl" of sand/beans for finger thrusts. "My gripping power and forearms have improved greatly." [LotD, Dec 18 1965 + Jan 1968 letters + editor's notes]

**Wing-chun-specific resistance:** 8 homemade iron rings, 3 lb each, worn on the forearms during sil lum tao. [LotD, 1965]

**Method changes after Wong Jack-man:** added weight training for strength, running for endurance, stretching for flexibility, fencing and boxing mechanics. [WP-BL "Fitness" section; quote "I realized Wing Chun was not too practical…" per Dorgan/Black Belt via Wikipedia "Wong Jack-man"]

**Warm-up doctrine (in his notes):** "To gain the greatest benefit… the exercises should imitate as closely as possible the movements which are to be used in the event." [ToJKD, Warming Up section]

**Mental training:** Dan Inosanto recalled meditation was the first item on Lee's schedule. [WP-BL, citing Thomas 1994 (Bruce Thomas, *Fighting Spirit*) p.97]

## 4. DIET (sourced)

- **Food = fuel doctrine:** a high-performance body is like a high-performance engine — "with 'the wrong fuel,' one's body would perform sluggishly or sloppily." [WP-BL, citing Little 1998 (AOEHB) p.162]
- **No empty calories:** avoided baked goods and refined flour — "empty calories that did nothing for his body." [WP-BL, citing Little 1998 p.163]
- **Chinese food over Western portions; vegetables, rice, fish, beef, shrimp, chicken, tofu;** liked steak; occasional McDonald's, but refused to binge junk. [Archived New Indian Express, "Fit as a fiddle: Bruce Lee's diet and fitness routine," Nov 28, 2018 — web.archive.org/web/20200728171336/…newindianexpress.com…1904397.html — the same article Wikipedia cites]
- **4–5 smaller meals a day** instead of three big ones; fruit through the day. [same archived article]
- **Hated dairy; used powdered milk** in cereal and protein shakes; put vegetables in his shakes. [same archived article + WP-BL]
- **Protein supplements confirmed in his own 1965 letters:** "As of today, I haven't yet received the protein but I'm sure it should arrive tomorrow or a day after. How much has it cost you?" [LotD, 1965 Hong Kong letter]
- **High-protein drinks + vitamin/mineral supplements** from soon after arriving in the US, per Linda Lee Cadwell. [WP-BL, citing Little 1998 p.162]
- **Tea, never coffee; normally consumed milk; "consumed only the proper food."** [BLFM p.16 — page image in refs]
- Congee, royal jelly, ginseng, exact shake recipes → UNVERIFIED section.

## 6. STORY BEATS (narrative arc)

1. **A superstitious start.** A boy born in San Francisco's Chinatown while his father tours with the opera, given a girl's name — "small phoenix" — to hide him from spirits. Raised in Hong Kong. (Beat lands on `photo_infant_3months.jpg` → `photo_1946_child_actor.jpg`.)
2. **Rooftop fights.** A teenage movie actor who keeps getting into street and rooftop fights — until he's sent to learn Wing Chun from Ip Man at 13 (per Linda's ToJKD intro). The other students refuse to train with the kid with European blood.
3. **Proof.** 1958: he KOs the schools boxing champion — and wins the colony cha-cha title the same year. Skill is skill.
4. **America with nothing.** 1959, Seattle, waiting tables; teaching anyone regardless of race — the thing that will later get him challenged.
5. **Long Beach, 1964.** Two fingers. One inch. Bob Baker stays home from work with chest pain. Hollywood is watching (Dozier calls).
6. **The fight that changed the training.** Wong Jack-man, Oakland, late 1964. Whatever really happened in those minutes, Lee walks away disgusted with his own conditioning — "unusually winded" — and rebuilds himself: weights, running, fencing, boxing. THE pivot of this video.
7. **The science era (the documents come alive).** 1965–68 letters: designing programs on paper from Hong Kong, iron rings, protein powder in the mail, wrist rollers from George Lee, "the hell with conventional methods." Jan 1968: 2.5 hours a day, logged. On-screen: the letters and the ToJKD circuit lists.
8. **The letter to himself.** January 1969 (kept secret): "I, Bruce Lee, will be the first highest paid Oriental super star…" — written by an out-of-work actor whose show was cancelled.
9. **Flat on his back.** 1970: severe back injury; doctors order him to stop training and stay in bed. Six months flat on his back — where he writes the notes that become *Tao of Jeet Kune Do*. The comeback fuel beat ("Walk on" as his aphorism — see caveat).
10. **Damn the torpedo.** 1971–72: Big Boss, Fist of Fury, Way of the Dragon — record after record; the Berton interview where the future superstar explains, in perfect English, why Hollywood won't cast him.
11. **32.** July 20, 1973. Six days before *Enter the Dragon* opens. Cerebral edema, still argued over.
12. **What he left.** Not a style — a paper trail: seven years of day planners, workout logs, tape measurements, letters, and a book written from a sickbed. The documents ARE the training program. (Close on the logs/AOEHB.)

**Three strongest hooks:** (1) the 1969 "Definite Chief Aim" letter — a broke actor writes himself a $10M prophecy and hits every line of it before dying at 32; (2) the Wong Jack-man fight — winning ugly is what turned a kung fu stylist into the first modern athlete of martial arts; (3) the back injury — the most famous body in martial arts was built twice, and his best-known book was written flat on his back.

## 7. UNVERIFIED — DO NOT USE (or fix source first)

Facts commonly repeated but NOT pinned to a checkable source this session:

1. **Sacral-nerve damage / "Good Morning" exercise with 135 lb as the injury cause / "doctors said he'd never kick again."** Attributed in secondary literature to Linda Lee's books and AOEHB ch.12; Matthew Polly (2018) disputes the doctor-prognosis part. Verified core (1970 injury, ordered to bed, 6 months, wrote ToJKD) is in Section 1; the *mechanism and prognosis details* are not. Fix: borrow AOEHB / Polly and pin pages.
2. **"Walk On" desk plaque story** (kept a card/plaque reading "Walk On" on his desk after the injury). The aphorism is his (ST, quote #7); the official site used "WALK ON!" as a section title (graphic saved), but the plaque anecdote itself is unpinned. Fix: Linda Lee, *The Man Only I Knew* (1975) or AOEHB.
3. **Congee with beef liver/beef as a staple; royal jelly + ginseng supplementation.** Standard AOEHB ch.10 material; the archived diet article and Wikipedia do not carry them. Do not state on-screen yet.
4. **Exact protein-shake recipe (raw eggs w/ shells, powdered milk, wheat germ, peanut butter, bananas…).** Powdered-milk shakes are verified (Section 4); the full recipe is AOEHB-only.
5. **The 1965 tape-measure chart values** (e.g., chest/biceps/forearm numbers) and **specific logged weights/reps** (squats, curls, "sequence training" day-planner logs 1965–1971). The circuits exist in ToJKD (verified); the *logged numbers* live in AOEHB's locked scans.
6. **Weight ~135–145 lb range.** 141 lb (64 kg) at 172 cm is verified [WP-BL]; the fuller range/fluctuation story (down to ~126 lb in 1973) is not.
7. **"Running 2–5 miles daily."** Verified: 4 miles/day in 24–25 min as "one of his favorite exercises" [BLFM]. The 2-and-5-mile bounds are AOEHB/secondary — use the 4-mile figure.
8. **"His ONLY English TV interview."** Wikipedia says "only known interview" from 1971 (Berton). He also gave other on-camera English interviews (e.g., 1960s Hong Kong TV clips circulate); phrase it as "his only surviving full-length talk-show interview" only after checking the Berton footage/its framing, or say "the longest sit-down interview he ever gave on camera" — safest: "the interview" without exclusivity claims.
9. **"Sickly child" framing.** Not sourced anywhere this session (skinny, street-fighting teen IS sourced). Polly describes a hyperactive, undersized kid. Avoid "sickly" unless pinned to Polly/Linda Lee pages.
10. **Two-finger push-up REP COUNTS and one-inch-punch distances beyond "approximately one inch."** Counts (e.g., "50 reps") circulate without primary sourcing.
11. **The famous lat-spread photos** (1970s, by Linda/John Little archive) are copyrighted Bruce Lee Enterprises material — fine to discuss, but licensing/fair-use review needed before showing; not saved to refs for that reason.
12. **jkd_manuscript item authenticity:** the 18-page typescript reads exactly like the essay published as "Liberate Yourself from Classical Karate" (*Black Belt*, Sept 1971) and carries his chop, but the archive.org uploader provenance is anonymous. Treat as "Lee's JKD typescript as circulated" and cross-check wording against the Black Belt article before claiming "his original manuscript" on screen.
