## 1. TIMELINE (era by era, with physique notes)

**1993 — born.** David Corenswet, born July 8, 1993, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. [WP-DC]

**~2011 — the Quaker-school acting teacher.** His high-school acting teacher Phil Brown was "the first teacher of mine who took me seriously enough to sort of call me on my high school bullshit… Don't let that be an excuse not to push yourself and not to work harder than everybody else." Brown was the last person Corenswet personally told he'd be Superman. [GQ-V, his own words]

**2016 — Juilliard.** BFA in drama, Juilliard School, class of 2016. [WP-DC] Movement training under Moni Yakim's famously brutal classes — "super high intensity almost to the point of being impossible… it became so directly transferable to the stunt work and the flying." [GQ-V — caption renders the name phonetically; see UNVERIFIED #9 for the spelling caveat]
- Physique note at this stage and through 2023: naturally lean; by his own account 190–195 lb on a 6'4" frame — "which is not very much for somebody who's 6'4"." [GQ-V]

**2019 — The Politician, and the rumor that started it all.** Netflix's *The Politician* (Ryan Murphy) makes him a face. [WP-DC] By late November 2019, Warner Bros. is *reportedly* listing him as a Cavill successor; Men's Health covers the rumor Dec 2, 2019, noting he had told Entertainment Weekly that playing Superman was his "pie-in-the-sky ambition" and that he wanted "an upbeat, throwback" version of the hero. [MH-19]

**2020 — Hollywood, and the interview that aged perfectly.** Ryan Murphy's *Hollywood* (Netflix, May 2020); Corenswet is de facto lead and executive producer. [WP-DC] In an April 2020 Men's Health interview he expands: "I just always felt like Superman was both under-appreciated and over-simplified… But that's all just in my mind." Told the interviewer could see him in the cape: "Great to know. I'll take that in writing. Maybe we can start a petition." [MH-25 — Romano republishing his own 2020 interview] He had trained with "all kinds of old-timey workouts" for the 1940s-set role. [MH-25]

**2022 — Pearl / We Own This City.** Ti West's *Pearl* (The Projectionist) and David Simon's *We Own This City*. [WP-DC; GQ-W calls him "best known for *Pearl* and David Simon's *We Own This City*"]

**June 2023 — the screen test and the two secrets.** June 17–18, 2023: final-round screen tests, three Clark/Lois pairs (Corenswet, Nicholas Hoult, Tom Brittney; Brosnahan, Mackey, Dynevor), suit-up on day two. [GQ-C, via THR] His wife Julia told him she was pregnant "two days after I found out I was going to screen test for *Superman*" — "we had these two quite huge secrets that we just had between the two of us. And that was intense." [PPL-C] Cast announced June 27, 2023. [WP-DC, citing Deadline] Gunn's call came while Corenswet was home on a break from filming *Twisters*: "It started with, 'Hey David, it's James Gunn.' **July 2023 – early 2024 — the bulk.** Training with LA trainer Paolo Mascitti begins July 2023, five to six days a week. [GQ-V, Corenswet recounting Gunn] Gunn picked Mascitti because he loved what Mascitti did with Flula Borg for *The Suicide Squad*. **2024 — filming, fatherhood, Twisters.** Filming underway from March 2024 [BI]; training drops to 3–4 sessions/week, still ~2 hours a day, half of it coached over FaceTime [GQ-W]. Julia's due date "originally coincided with when he was set to start filming in early 2024… It was very close." [PPL-C] *Twisters* releases July 2024 (he plays storm chaser Scott). ## 3. TRAINING SYSTEM (the reported program, with numbers)

**The team & the brief.** Trainer: Paolo Mascitti, Los Angeles/Beverly Hills. Hired on Gunn's recommendation after his *Suicide Squad* work with Flula Borg; also trained Tom Ellis, Christian Slater, Nicole Scherzinger. [GQ-W] Mascitti's own bio: 20+ years training, former professional volleyball player. [TWP] Gunn's brief per Mascitti: "This is a James Gunn film, and he has a specific vision of the entire project… He's 6'4" but we wanted to put more bulk on him." [GQ-W] Look target per Mascitti: "more like a farm boy than a complete bodybuilder." [BI]

**Block length & frequency.**
- Started July 2023, 5–6 days/week through the bulk. [BI]
- Mascitti to GQ: they "began getting down to it five months before production began"; during filming (from March 2024 [BI]) it dropped to "three or four sessions a week, but he's still working out for two hours a day," half coached remotely over FaceTime. [GQ-W]
- Corenswet's own math: screen-test suit at 195 lb → "I showed up four months later, five months later… 240 lb." [GQ-V]

**The split.** Classic push / pull / legs, rinse and repeat. Push day = presses etc.; pull day = pull-ups, pull-downs, rows; leg day = squats. [BI; GQ-W] "We focus on… presses, pull ups, pull downs, rows—all the big ones. We switched a little bit after a while, but kept the focus on progressive overload… keeping track of reps and weight and trying to advance each week." [GQ-W, Mascitti]

**Loading scheme.** Progressive overload; compound lifts first plus some isolation; rep range usually 6–12, pushed to 15–20 on isolation moves (e.g. cable flies) to fatigue the muscle at session end. [BI] Mascitti's doctrine: "it's not about showy moves, it's about mastering the exercise and focusing on things like full range of motion, mastering the negative, stretching, and trying to progress in weight and reps." [GQ-W]

**Core/stunt work.** "A lot of core work" specifically to support stunts and harness work. [BI] Corenswet credits Juilliard movement training for the harness/flying positions ("strung up by my underpants and had to hold my limbs and my torso in this steady exact position"). [GQ-V]

**The published sample push day (Mascitti's, as printed in GQ-W — 4 sets × 10 reps, last set to failure optional):**
1. Incline dumbbell press (bench ~45°)
2. Bench press superset with cable fly
3. Seated overhead shoulder press superset with lateral raise
4. Reverse-grip triceps extension superset with biceps curl

**The published second session (GQ-W's "Hoult-style" cardio-leaning day — 4×10 unless stated):** squats; reverse-to-forward lunge superset with 30 lb kettlebell (10/leg); leg raises/extensions on machine; "CrossFit sit-ups" max reps in 60 s; 3-minute all-out incline run (up to 5 sprint blocks across the workout).

**Known weakness:** walking lunges. "He isn't the biggest fan of them, but he does it. He's great at squats, but he just doesn't enjoy lunges." [GQ-W, Mascitti]

**Result:** 195 → 240 lb at peak per Corenswet [GQ-V]; ~200 → 240 per Mascitti, with mass intentionally shed as he toned toward camera [GQ-W]; "40 pounds" is the number both People and BI ran, "45lbs" is GQ's video description. Not "shredded" by design — buff, not ripped. [BI]

## 4. DIET (sourced)

- **Calorie target — his words:** "I was aiming for about 4,500 calories a day. The gym was the first place I went to prepare." [GQ-V]
- **Calorie progression — trainer's words to BI:** gradually built UP to 4,500/day during the bulk; dropped to ~3,500/day (slight deficit) as filming approached to shed body fat. [BI]
- **Conflicting trainer number:** Mascitti told GQ "He was on around 6,000 calories a day and his diet was strict, but not as strict as I would have liked." [GQ-W] → On screen, attribute numbers to their speaker ("Corenswet says he aimed for 4,500; Mascitti has put the peak as high as 6,000") — do not average or pick one silently.
- **Macros:** 50% carbs / 30% protein / 20% fat. [BI]
- **Protein:** 0.75–1 g per lb bodyweight daily, from eggs, dairy, chicken, red meat, fish, whey. [BI]
- **Carb sources:** rice, oats, sweet potatoes, vegetables; higher-carb meals placed before workouts. [BI]
- **Fat sources:** olive oil, avocado, seeds. [BI]
- **Meal frequency: 7 meals/eating occasions a day.** Sample breakfast: 6 egg whites + 2 whole eggs, 1.5–2 cups oatmeal, berries, Greek yogurt with almonds, plus a protein shake. Other meals: rice + beef/chicken/turkey/fish + vegetables (broccoli) + shake; snacks of shakes or unsweetened Greek yogurt with berries. [BI]
- **Quality rule:** mostly whole, unprocessed foods — "the quality of calories was as much a priority as the quantity" — with occasional French toast and waffles. [BI]
- **The kryptonite:** "He loves cereal, that's his kryptonite." [BI, Mascitti] Same story to GQ: "We'd speak remotely and I'd ask him about his diet and he'd be eating cereal! He'd ask *What's wrong with cereal!?*" [GQ-W]
- **Sleep** explicitly programmed as the third pillar: "Everything is part of the puzzle. You take one of the things out and it's not going to happen." [BI, Mascitti]

## 6. STORY BEATS (narrative arc)

1. **The petition, 2019.** A 26-year-old from *The Politician* tells Entertainment Weekly that Superman is his "pie-in-the-sky ambition" — while Henry Cavill is still publicly refusing to let go of the cape. Men's Health writes it up as a rumor. (On screen: `doc_mh_2019-12-02_superman_rumor_article.png`.)
2. **"I'll take that in writing," 2020.** In an April 2020 interview he lays out his whole thesis — Superman is "under-appreciated and over-simplified" — and jokes about starting a petition. The interviewer republishes the receipts five years later. (`doc_mh_2025-07-16_romano_destiny_article.png`.)
3. **Two secrets, June 2023.** Two days after he learns he'll screen test, his wife tells him she's pregnant. Nobody can know either thing. Then an unknown 310 number calls: "Can you prove that?" (`doc_people_2025-06-20_screentest_pregnancy_article.png`.)
4. **The brief.** Gunn's actual instruction, in Corenswet's telling: get a trainer, "work on your shoulders and your vulnerability." Gunn hand-picks Mascitti off his *Suicide Squad* work. The measurement on the suit order: 6'4", 195 lb.
5. **The lonely math of the bulk.** July 2023 onward: push/pull/legs 5–6 days a week, 4,500 calories, seven meals, jazz in his headphones — and his own line about the ninth rep being something nobody can do for you. Wife and husband gain 40 lb each in the same months; he ends up on her pregnancy pillow. THE central training act of the video.
6. **The squeeze.** Four-to-five months later he walks into the fitting at 240 lb and doesn't fit in the suit built for 195. The costume department's tape measure is the objective proof of the transformation — better than any before/after photo.
7. **Superman with a bruise.** The flying leap in the trailer is also the take where Superman bruises a testicle — his willingness to tell it on a polygraph is the character note (humility) that doubles as the physique note (the stunts were real loads).
8. **July 11, 2025.** ~$220M global opening. The 2019 "petition" joke completes as prophecy. And the plan was never to look like a bodybuilder — "a farm boy, not a complete bodybuilder" is the thesis of the whole physique.
9. **Do it again, 2026–27.** *Man of Tomorrow* announced Sept 2025, cameras rolling April 20, 2026, release July 9, 2027 — the maintenance era (how he keeps 6'4"/~230+ between films is an open question we can pose, not answer — see UNVERIFIED #8).

**Three strongest hooks:** (1) the paper trail of destiny — a 2019 Men's Health rumor piece and a 2020 "let's start a petition" quote, both archived, published years before the June 2023 casting; (2) the suit built for a 195-lb man that a 240-lb man came back to wear — the transformation measured not in gym selfies but by a costume fitting; (3) the double-40 — his wife gained ~40 lb pregnant while he gained ~40 lb for the role in the same months, the most human "bulking" story any superhero actor has told.

## 7. UNVERIFIED — DO NOT USE (or fix source first)

1. **"195 lb → 235–240 lb" as a "~6-month bulk."** The endpoints ARE verified (195 and 240, his own words [GQ-V]), but no source says six months: Corenswet says the fitting was "four months later, five months later" [GQ-V]; Mascitti says they started "five months before production" [GQ-W]; BI's dates (training from July 2023, filming from March 2024) imply ~8 months. Say "in a matter of months" or "by the first fitting, four to five months later."
2. **"He ate every 2 hours."** Not found in any checkable source this session. What IS sourced: seven meals/eating occasions a day [BI]. Use the 7-meals figure.
