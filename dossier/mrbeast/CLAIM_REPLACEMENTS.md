# MrBeast Documentary — Claim-Safe Replacement Lines

Copy-ready language for every **WEAK** or **FAIL** item in
`CLAIM_AUDIT.md`. These lines prioritize accuracy over dramatic compression.
They may be shortened only if attribution and evidentiary limits remain intact.

## Opening and adolescent chronology

### Exact teenage image / age / baseball / audience composite

**Approved default**

> In the archive, Jimmy is a teenager—still talking about baseball and still
> uploading to a tiny audience. He later said he was diagnosed with Crohn's
> disease at fifteen.

**Approved only after the selected upload is date-verified**

> This upload appeared while Jimmy was fifteen. He was still playing baseball,
> still making videos, and—by his later account—about to lose the physical life
> he knew to Crohn's disease.

**Do not use**

> His body has begun to turn against him.

unless the upload's recording date is demonstrably within the symptom period.

**Do not use**

> An audience small enough to fit inside a classroom.

unless contemporaneous subscriber data supports a specific number.

### Weight and muscle

> Jimmy says he fell from about 190 pounds to 139—roughly fifty pounds—and
> lost the muscle he had built for baseball.

Short form:

> By Jimmy's account, his weight fell from about 190 pounds to 139.

### Baseball-to-YouTube causality

> In Jimmy's telling, the illness ended the college-baseball path and pushed
> him all-in on YouTube.

Follow with:

> Crohn's did not create his success. But it helped redirect his time and
> identity toward the one pursuit that remained.

### Title thesis

Use near the start to qualify the title:

> Crohn's did not build MrBeast by itself. But Jimmy says it closed one path
> just as he committed everything to another.

Use in the ending:

> The disease did not create his discipline or guarantee his success. It
> changed the terrain on which both developed.

## Medical language

### Subject's "gut attacks itself" shorthand

Immediately after the soundbite:

> That is Jimmy's shorthand. NIDDK describes Crohn's as chronic
> digestive-tract inflammation in which genes, abnormal immune reactions, and
> the microbiome may all play a role.

Shorter approved version:

> That is Jimmy's shorthand. Medically, Crohn's is a chronic inflammatory
> disease whose causes are more complex.

### Treatment and immune-system claim

Introduction:

> Jimmy describes the burden of his treatment this way.

After the bite, if clarification is needed:

> Immunosuppressants and biologic medicines are among the treatments used for
> Crohn's, but Jimmy does not identify his medication here.

Do not narrate or graph:

> His treatment shuts down his immune system.

That language may appear only inside Jimmy's own sourced quotation.

### Diet and absorption

> Dietary needs can change with symptoms, medicines, and problems absorbing
> nutrients. No particular diet is known to cure IBD.

### Illness interrupting fitness

General, evidence-safe:

> Crohn's can still interrupt a plan without permission.

Do not use:

> Crohn's interrupted Jimmy's program.

unless a first-person example is added to the evidence bank.

## Work, psychology, and control

### Production superlatives

Replace:

> The most productive creator alive.

with:

> The creator behind one of YouTube's largest production operations.

Use the narrower version if production scale is not quantified onscreen:

> The creator behind a tightly controlled production operation.

### Obsession and measurable feedback

> YouTube gave that obsession measurable feedback—and, by Jimmy's account,
> rewarded more of it.

### Accountability as choice, not psychological necessity

> He chose to make someone else part of the system.

Expanded:

> Rather than trust motivation, he chose to make another person part of the
> system—someone who would notice if he stopped.

### Channel and self-care

> He realized his focus on the channel had left him not working out or taking
> care of himself.

Do not use:

> The channel had consumed too much of his health.

### Interpretive terrain language

> The physical path narrowed. YouTube absorbed more of his time and identity.

This is preferable to saying the digital path absorbed "everything."

## Contract and chronology

### Day 310

> In June 2023, Jimmy said he was on day 310 of a workout pact with Eric
> Decker, better known as Airrack.

> Programmed rest days counted. The promise was to follow the plan and hold
> each other accountable—not to complete 310 consecutive workouts.

### Tattoo terms

> Jimmy said breaking the pact meant getting a tattoo of the other person.

If using Airrack's forehead line:

> Airrack said his side of the agreement required MrBeast's name tattooed on
> his forehead if he missed a day.

Do not claim both men faced identical forehead-tattoo terms without the
contract.

### "Legally binding"

Narration:

> Airrack called it a legally binding contract.

Lower-third:

> AIRRACK'S DESCRIPTION — CONTRACT NOT SHOWN IN FULL

### 310-to-600 chronology

**Mandatory replacement**

> Jimmy described the pact on day 310 without naming its endpoint. In
> Airrack's later film, Eric described it as a 600-day bodybuilding challenge.

Shorter:

> On day 310, Jimmy did not name an endpoint. Airrack's later film described
> the full challenge as 600 days.

Do not use:

> The agreement later expanded.

or:

> The agreement later evolved.

without new primary evidence.

### 602-day finish

> Airrack says the project took 602 days to film.

or:

> The pact ran across roughly 600 days, with programmed rest allowed.

Do not use:

> They worked out for 602 consecutive days.

### Scope of Airrack film

> Airrack's film documents his side of the challenge: trainers, travel,
> setbacks, and expectations that changed as the months accumulated.

Do not assign Airrack's meals, workout plan, coaching changes, or setbacks to
Jimmy.

## Workout, movement, food, and body composition

### High-level workout description

> The verified record supports consistent resistance training with a trainer.
> It does not supply a complete Jimmy-specific split, set count, or exercise
> list.

### Completeness limitation

> In the sources we could verify, Jimmy never gives a complete workout split,
> meal plan, or calorie target.

This is safer than claiming no such information exists anywhere.

### 41% body-fat figure

Narration:

> Jimmy says he started at 41 percent body fat.

Graphic:

> 41%  
> JIMMY'S STATED STARTING FIGURE  
> MEASUREMENT METHOD NOT PUBLIC

Do not show bare `41%` without the attribution line.

### 40%+ to sub-20%

**Only after a defensible primary capture is archived**

> In June 2023, Jimmy reported going from more than 40 percent body fat to
> below 20 percent. He did not publish the measurement method with the post.

Without primary capture:

> Jimmy publicly described major fat loss, but the verified interviews do not
> provide a complete set of comparable measurements.

### 15,000 steps

> In a June 2023 interview, Jimmy said he was walking 15,000 steps a day and
> taking phone calls while he walked.

### 12,500 steps

**Only after a defensible primary capture is archived**

> In a separate June 2023 transformation post, Jimmy credited lifting and
> walking 12,500 steps a day.

If the primary post cannot be archived, omit the number. Do not substitute a
secondary article in a primary-evidence graphic.

### Ninety minutes and at least two hours

> Jimmy estimated the workout at about 90 minutes and said the full routine
> redirected at least two hours of his day from the main channel.

Graphic:

> ~90 MIN — WORKOUT  
> ≥2 HOURS — TOTAL TIME REDIRECTED FROM MAIN CHANNEL  
> JIMMY'S JUNE 2023 ESTIMATE

Do not graph `90 minutes + steps + food = two hours` as an exact equation.

### Food

Card title:

> FOOD: DETAILS NOT PUBLIC

Narration based only on the acquired interview:

> Jimmy counted food among the routine's time costs, but the interview does
> not reveal a meal plan or calorie target.

Expanded version, only after archiving the June 2023 primary post:

> Jimmy counted food among the routine's time costs and later credited a good
> diet, but he did not publish enough detail to reproduce the plan.

### Final factual payoff

**Approved without the missing social posts**

> The record shows sustained lifting, high daily step counts, a trainer, and
> an accountability pact. Jimmy says he lost fat, but his exact measurements
> and complete program are not public.

**Approved after archiving the relevant June 2023 primary posts**

> Jimmy publicly credited his progress to months of lifting, high daily step
> counts, sleep, and diet. The interviews also document a trainer and an
> accountability pact. His measurement methods and complete program are not
> public.

### Progress versus expectation

> By the end, Jimmy said the challenge had improved their lives—but also that
> he had made far less progress than he expected.

### Crohn's payoff boundary

> Fitness gave him more agency over his habits. It did not cure Crohn's or
> guarantee what his body would do next.

## Strict script-lock checklist

A reviewer must answer **YES** to every item. Any **NO** blocks narration lock.

### Source identity and chronology

- [ ] Every interview bite ID exists in `mrbeast_soundbites.json`.
- [ ] Every bite used in the locked cut has transcript verification.
- [ ] Every selected source window has frame verification.
- [ ] The opening teenage upload has a source ID and upload date.
- [ ] Any claim about age at the moment of the opening footage is supported.
- [ ] Upload date is not silently treated as recording date.
- [ ] The opening audience-size claim has contemporaneous evidence or is
      removed.
- [ ] The JRE original-release/re-upload history is resolved before any date is
      shown.
- [ ] The script does not say the 310-day pact later expanded/evolved.
- [ ] The 600-day claim is attributed to Airrack's later film.
- [ ] The script distinguishes project duration from consecutive workouts.
- [ ] Programmed rest is explicitly preserved.

### Medical safety

- [ ] First diagnosis mention says Jimmy **says** he was diagnosed at 15.
- [ ] Symptom frequency, pain, weight loss, and treatment burden are presented
      as Jimmy's account.
- [ ] Jimmy's "gut attacks itself" wording is not used as the clinical
      definition.
- [ ] The medical definition is sourced to NIDDK.
- [ ] No narration claims fitness improved, controlled, treated, or cured his
      Crohn's.
- [ ] Diet language states that needs may vary and no diet cures IBD.
- [ ] No unidentified stock infusion or hospital footage is presented as
      Jimmy's treatment.
- [ ] No medication is named or inferred without a primary source.
- [ ] The film states that it is biography, not treatment guidance.

### Metrics and attribution

- [ ] `190 → 139` is introduced as Jimmy's report.
- [ ] "Lost all muscle" is not presented literally.
- [ ] `41%` is labeled as Jimmy's stated figure.
- [ ] `41%` discloses that the measurement method is not public.
- [ ] `sub-20%` appears only if the primary post is archived.
- [ ] `12,500 steps` appears only if the primary post is archived.
- [ ] `15,000 steps` is dated to the June 2023 interview.
- [ ] `90 minutes` is identified as Jimmy's estimate.
- [ ] `at least two hours` is described as time redirected from the main
      channel, not an exact physiological-work equation.
- [ ] Every numeric graphic shows source and date.
- [ ] No body-fat percentage is inferred from photographs.

### Workout and diet

- [ ] The script limits the workout description to resistance training with a
      trainer unless new primary evidence is added.
- [ ] No invented split, exercises, sets, reps, calories, macros, or meal plan.
- [ ] Airrack's workout and diet are not assigned to Jimmy.
- [ ] Jimmy's coach is not named without primary attribution.
- [ ] The food section says the reproducible details are not public.
- [ ] The final payoff does not claim a proven multi-factor causal recipe.
- [ ] All transformation outcomes remain explicitly self-reported where
      objective measurements are absent.

### Interpretation and rhetoric

- [ ] "The most productive creator alive" is removed.
- [ ] The film does not diagnose Jimmy from his use of "hyper-obsession."
- [ ] "Needed someone else" is replaced with a choice/accountability framing.
- [ ] "Channel consumed his health" is replaced with his narrower self-care
      statement.
- [ ] The title thesis is explicitly complicated; disease is not declared the
      sole cause of success.
- [ ] Interpretive narration is distinguishable from recorded fact.
- [ ] The ending says fitness created agency, not biological control.

### Final rendered-narration audit

- [ ] Generate a transcript from the actual final narration/audio assembly.
- [ ] Compare that transcript line by line with `CLAIM_AUDIT.md`.
- [ ] Confirm no alternate take reintroduced a rejected phrase.
- [ ] Confirm on-screen text obeys the same attribution rules as narration.
- [ ] Confirm lower thirds do not elevate a subject's claim into a medical or
      independently measured fact.
- [ ] Re-run the claim audit after any substantive narration edit.

**Lock rule:** all boxes must be checked, or a documented new primary source
must explain and support the exception.
